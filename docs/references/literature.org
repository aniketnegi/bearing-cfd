* Gangrade, Phalle, and Mantha

Ajay Kumar Gangrade, Vikas M. Phalle, and S. S. Mantha,
“Performance Analysis of a Conical Hydrodynamic Journal Bearing,” /Iranian
Journal of Science and Technology, Transactions of Mechanical Engineering/,
volume 43, pages 559--573; published online in 2018.

- DOI: [[https://doi.org/10.1007/s40997-018-0217-2][10.1007/s40997-018-0217-2]]
- Local author copy:
  [[file:../../../../docs/papers_sorted/conical-journal-bearing/cfd/Performance Analysis of a Conical Hydrodynamic Journal Bearing.pdf][Performance Analysis of a Conical Hydrodynamic Journal Bearing.pdf]]

** Relevance to this project

The paper analyzes the lubricant clearance using a modified Reynolds equation
in a conical/spherical-coordinate formulation and solves the resulting
thin-film problem by finite elements on a developed conical surface.  It
motivates the periodic circumferential/axial master coordinates and the
thin-film context.  Its numerical results are compared with ANSYS Fluent and
its 10-degree, aspect-ratio-one case is compared with five experimental
pressure taps.  This makes it the closest target for conical pressure-field
validation, but not for calibrated three-dimensional cavitation.

The final circular O-grid Fluent candidate now matches its 100 mm length,
50 mm mean radius, 50 micrometre radial clearance, and 10-degree semi-cone
angle.  The paper does not establish the operating temperature, exact =V-32=
product, pressure reference, complete three-dimensional feed treatment, or
liquid--vapour/air inputs.  It therefore does not validate the local bore-patch
feed representation or supply a Fluent cavitation material set.

The publisher's current journal page explicitly lists the journal in the
Science Citation Index Expanded:
[[https://link.springer.com/journal/40997][Springer journal record]].

* Three-dimensional CFD and cavitation shortlist

Journal indexing is not enough by itself: a usable validation target must also
disclose compatible geometry, materials, boundaries, model equations, and
comparison quantities.  No inspected paper supplies all of those for the
present smooth eccentric conical oil bearing.

| Role | Source | What it can check | Strict limitation |
|---+---+---+---|
| Primary conical pressure target | Gangrade, Phalle, and Mantha, [[https://doi.org/10.1007/s40997-018-0217-2][10.1007/s40997-018-0217-2]] | Matching core geometry, liquid baseline, normalized maximum-pressure curve, and experimental circumferential pressure comparison | Main model is modified Reynolds/FEM; temperature, phase/gas properties, pressure reference, and reproducible 3-D cavitation setup are absent |
| Secondary 3-D conical-method target | Vaziri et al., “Numerical analysis of conical hydrodynamic bearing lubricated with magnetorheological fluid,” [[https://doi.org/10.1007/s40430-023-04616-w][10.1007/s40430-023-04616-w]] | Full three-dimensional conical CFD/FEM practice and comparisons with earlier analytical, numerical, and experimental data | Magnetorheological non-Newtonian fluid and no cavitation; do not transfer its material parameters |
| Cavitation-physics target | Song and Gu, “Development and Validation of a Three-Dimensional Computational Fluid Dynamics Analysis for Journal Bearings Considering Cavitation and Conjugate Heat Transfer,” [[https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4030633][10.1115/1.4030633]] | Three-dimensional journal-bearing CFD comparison of Half-Sommerfeld, vapour, and gaseous cavitation against experiments | Cylindrical two-groove bearing, not conical; reproduce its own case before using it as method evidence |
| Closest topical support | Chen et al., “Multi-objective analysis design of spiral groove conical bearing considering cavitation effects,” [[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rineng.2022.100582][10.1016/j.rineng.2022.100582]] | Multiphase cavitation in a conical bearing and a reported maximum 6.63% deviation from published test data | Spiral-groove geometry; strict SCIE status was not verified from a primary index record, so it is not the project's SCI anchor |

** Hierarchical validation rule

Use independent evidence for independent claims:

1. reproduce the Gangrade normalized maximum-pressure curve and pressure-tap
   comparison as far as its disclosed conditions permit;
2. reproduce a fully specified Song--Gu cavitation case to check the
   three-dimensional multiphase procedure;
3. apply the checked procedure to the present conical mesh and report model,
   property, mesh, timestep, and boundary-condition sensitivity;
4. call the combined conical-cavitation result directly validated only after a
   matching experiment or equally specific independent dataset is available.

Agreement with one scalar, solver convergence, or transferring another
bearing's phase properties is not validation.

** Physical interpretation sources

- Li et al., “Cavitation Mechanism of Oil-Film Bearing and Development of a
  New Gaseous Cavitation Model Based on Air Solubility,”
  [[https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4006702][10.1115/1.4006702]], supports treating
  dissolved-air release as distinct from pure oil vapour.
- Shen and Khonsari, “On the Magnitude of Cavitation Pressure of Steady-State
  Lubrication,” [[https://doi.org/10.1007/s11249-013-0158-2][10.1007/s11249-013-0158-2]],
  shows that effective lubricant-film cavitation pressure depends on dissolved
  gas and operating conditions and is not one universal material constant.

  (1981), 350--354:
  [[https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3251669][10.1115/1.3251669]].
- Giacopini et al., “A Mass-Conserving Complementarity Formulation to Study
  Lubricant Films in the Presence of Cavitation,” /Journal of Tribology/ 132
  (2010), 041702:
  [[https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4002215][10.1115/1.4002215]].
- Miraskari et al., “A Robust Modification to the Universal Cavitation
  Algorithm in Journal Bearings,” /Journal of Tribology/ 139 (2017), 031703:
  [[https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4034244][10.1115/1.4034244]].

The local automated test checks broad graph-read bands around the 60-cell
sinusoidal slider peaks and rupture/reformation locations shown by Miraskari et
al.  Their compressible Elrod calculation uses a 100 GPa bulk modulus and is
compared with Giacopini's incompressible LCP; the local test directly assembles
that incompressible limit.  This is a coarse check of the shared active-set
kernel, not a digitized pressure-profile comparison or validation of the
conical model.  Exact inputs and results are recorded in
[[file:../handoff/15-jfo-validation-and-fluent.org][JFO validation and Fluent path]].

* Manufactured solution and grid convergence

Gravenkamp, Pfeil, and Codina publish a Reynolds--Elrod manufactured solution
and its forcing data:

- paper:
  [[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2023.116488][10.1016/j.cma.2023.116488]];
- code/data:
  [[https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8315789][10.5281/zenodo.8315789]].

Reusing it requires a separate diagnostic mode with the published smooth
regularization and forcing.  The paper's second-order result applies to its
stabilized linear finite elements; its artificial-diffusion result is first
order, which is the relevant initial expectation for the current upwind finite
volume scheme.  The local observed order must still be measured.  Grid
convergence must then follow the procedure of Celik et al.,
[[https://doi.org/10.1115/1.2960953][10.1115/1.2960953]], after the physical feed
footprint is made consistent across refinements.
