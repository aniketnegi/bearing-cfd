* Verdict

#+begin_quote
The shared active-set complementarity kernel is consistent with graph-read
peaks and fronts from a published 60-cell slider comparison.  The 2000-rpm
conical result remains an unvalidated numerical candidate because the feed
footprint is grid-dependent, the native conical assembly has not passed an
external solution, and the physical cavity mechanism has not been fixed as
vapour formation or air ventilation.
#+end_quote

The reported =64.455%= is high.  It is surface area with
=thetaFill < 1 - 1e-8=, not 64.455% vapour volume.  The lower fill thresholds
show that the depletion is real inside the current model, but neither that
metric nor the prescribed 101,325 Pa floor can be compared directly with
Fluent vapour volume fraction.

No single inspected paper supplies the target geometry, exact oil at a stated
temperature, complete phase/gas properties, a calibrated Fluent model, and
matching cavity measurements.  Quantitative evidence must therefore be
stacked rather than attributed to one nominally similar paper.

* Why Reynolds and why JFO

| Layer | Meaning | Current role |
|---+---+---|
| Reynolds equation | Thin-film mass conservation and pressure equation derived under lubrication assumptions | Governing equation on the unwrapped conical film |
| Simple rupture boundary | Clips or terminates the pressure solution where it would become nonphysical | Cheap, but generally loses mass and does not handle reformation correctly |
| JFO conditions | Jakobsson--Floberg--Olsson mass-conserving rupture and reformation law | Declares full-film pressure cells and partially filled rupture cells |
| Elrod--Adams/complementarity | Numerical formulations for solving Reynolds plus JFO | The current solver uses an active-set complementarity form |
| Fluent cavitation model | 3-D liquid--vapour mass transfer derived from Rayleigh--Plesset bubble dynamics | A different physical model and an independent comparison |

The Reynolds reduction is appropriate when film thickness is much smaller
than bearing radius and length and the requested outputs are film pressure,
load, leakage, and torque.  It avoids resolving many cells through a
50-micrometre gap.  It is not sufficient by itself after a full-film solution
demands negative absolute pressure.

JFO was used instead of pressure clipping because it conserves lubricant mass
through rupture and reformation.  It is not universally “better.”  Use:

- thermohydrodynamic Reynolds analysis when viscosity and temperature feedback
  matter;
- elastohydrodynamic analysis when surface deformation matters;
- 3-D liquid--vapour cavitation when oil phase change is the mechanism;
- oil--air VOF or a related interface model when the open ends ventilate the
  film.

The fine sensitivity is encouraging, but it is not a formal three-grid
convergence result.  The feed geometry does not form a consistent refinement
family, and the coarse/intermediate sequence oscillates.  No observed order,
Richardson extrapolation, or GCI is reported from those grids.

The defensible fix is a feed boundary whose physical area and location are
preserved during refinement, preferably a conforming or locally refined
circular footprint.  Only after that should the project calculate GCI for
pressure, load components, feed flow, torque, cavity area, and front position
using [[https://doi.org/10.1115/1.2960953][the Celik et al. procedure]].

* Physical-model sensitivity

The current inputs set both axial ambient pressure and JFO rupture pressure to
101,325 Pa absolute.  This imposes an ambient-pressure rupture floor and is
consistent with an assumed ventilated interpretation.  The solver has no air
phase, atmospheric-connectivity model, or gas transport, so it does not itself
prove or simulate ventilation.

Changing only the rupture pressure to an illustrative 0.5 Pa in the 128 by 40
case changed cavity area from 64.485% to 53.879% and maximum absolute pressure
from 17.926 MPa to 18.848 MPa.  This does not establish 0.5 Pa as a correct oil
property.  It demonstrates that the predicted cavity depends materially on
whether the model assumes an ambient-pressure rupture or an oil-vapour floor.

The axial conditions also fix pressure at the rupture value while extrapolating
fill.  This is consistent with a vented/starved-end interpretation and can
allow the rupture region to connect to both ends, but it does not transport an
air phase.  Flooded or oil-wetted ends require a different, explicit
replenishment condition.

* Remaining numerical acceptance gates

Before presenting the conical outputs as validated:

1. preserve the feed geometry across refinement and obtain monotonic
   grid-convergence evidence;
2. exercise the native OpenFOAM conical assembly against an exact or published
   field, not only the Python LCP kernel;
3. implement a separate forced, smoothly regularized diagnostic using the
   published Reynolds--Elrod manufactured solution from
   [[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2023.116488][Gravenkamp, Pfeil, and Codina]]
   and its [[https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8315789][forcing data]], then measure
   the local observed order; the paper's second-order result belongs to its
   stabilized FEM, while its artificial-diffusion scheme is first order and is
   the relevant initial expectation for this upwind FVM;
4. report local control-volume imbalance as well as global flow imbalance;
5. report pressure, fill, and normalized complementarity residual bounds;
6. declare and test flooded versus vented ends, feed representation, rupture
   pressure, initial fill, and continuation sensitivity;
7. compare pressure profiles, fronts, load, flow, and torque with independent
   numerical or experimental data—not only one peak-pressure scalar.

For every accepted field require:

\[
p-p_\mathrm{cav}\ge0,\qquad 0\le\theta\le1,\qquad
(p-p_\mathrm{cav})(1-\theta)=0,
\]

plus conservative flux balance and stable integrated outputs.

** What went wrong

The reduced Reynolds/JFO mesh was unwrapped to a plane and extruded through one
arbitrary 1 mm pseudo-thickness cell so OpenFOAM could carry a two-dimensional
film equation.  Its minimum OQ =0.956754= was valid for that reduced numerical
mesh only.  We failed to keep that diagnostic explicitly separated from the
required physical 3-D Fluent mesh, and then treated its quality as predictive
of the 3-D sweep.  That was a scope and acceptance mistake, not evidence that
Fluent should be run in 2-D.

The physical sweep spans a 20--80 micrometre radial gap; its surface-normal
component is approximately 19.7--78.8 micrometres because of the 10-degree
cone angle.  It uses 12 real cells through the thickness.  Curved wall faces
and micrometre-scale cell-centroid offsets alter the face-normal, cell-to-face,
and cell-to-neighbour alignment used by Fluent OQ.  Therefore OQ fell even
though the surface topology itself was valid.

The correction sequence was:

| Mesh | Minimum OQ | Decision |
|---+---:|---|
| Reduced planar JFO pseudo-layer | 0.956754 | valid only for the reduced model; rejected as 3-D evidence |
| 256 by 80 by 12 physical sweep | 0.461367 | rejected |
| 512 by 160 by 12 physical sweep | 0.890532 | rejected by the requested 0.9 gate |
| 576 by 180 by 12 physical sweep | 0.922660 | accepted statically |

At 512 by 160, all 15,142 sub-0.9 cells were in the unchanged background
block, not the circular inlet O-grid, and they spanned all 12 gap layers.  The
worst cell was in gap layer 0 at the first axial row; its limiting term was
cell-to-face-centroid alignment on a wrapped constant-gap internal face.
Refining the complete surface reduced that curved-span/centroid offset;
inlet-only reshaping could not have fixed the global minimum.  The old exporter
also still carried a 0.8 publication threshold after the target changed to
0.9.  The native writer now receives the requested threshold explicitly and
refuses publication below it.

* How to run the final Fluent comparison

** Validation target and evidence stack

The literature roles are ranked in
[[file:../references/literature.org][the literature map]].  The practical
validation hierarchy is:

1. *Conical pressure target*: reproduce Gangrade et al.'s normalized
   maximum-pressure curve and its five-tap circumferential pressure comparison
   as far as the disclosed conditions permit.  The final mesh matches the
   selected 100 mm length, 50 mm mean radius, 50 micrometre radial clearance,
   and 10-degree semi-cone geometry.
2. *Cavitation-method target*: obtain and reproduce one fully specified
   Song--Gu three-dimensional journal-bearing case using that paper's own
   geometry, lubricant, temperature, and vapour/gas treatment.
3. *Application case*: apply the checked procedure to the conical mesh and
   report material, model-form, mesh, timestep, scheme, initialization, and
   boundary-condition sensitivities.
4. *Direct combined validation*: reserve this label for comparison with a
   matching conical-cavitation experiment or equally specific independent
   dataset.

The direct paper's =2.6 m/s= corresponds to about =496.6 rpm= at 50 mm radius,
whereas its validation figure states =2000 rpm=.  They are separate points.
Its =0.5 MPa= reference, exact test-oil temperature, and load-controlled
experimental equilibrium geometry are also insufficiently specified.  These
limitations must remain attached to any pressure-curve comparison.

** Near-term defensible route: independent 3-D physical model

Do not call Fluent's built-in cavitation model “the same JFO calculation.”
Ansys documents Schnerr--Sauer and Zwart--Gerber--Belamri as liquid--vapour
mass-transfer models based on Rayleigh--Plesset bubble dynamics:
[[https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v261/en/flu_th/flu_th_sec_multiphase_cavitation.html][Fluent cavitation theory]].

Use the statically audited 3-D film/feed mesh only after it passes the
following gates in order.

*** Acceptance ladder

1. *Live mesh*: 3-D double precision import, Mesh Check, Report Quality, units,
   bounds, connected-region count, and exact zone/count audit.
2. *Zero-flow identity*: single liquid, journal at 0 rpm, all openings
   atmospheric; require the initialized ambient, zero-velocity state to remain
   unchanged.
3. *Separate commissioning cases*: restart from the zero-flow identity for
   the pressure-fed 0-rpm case and separately for the zero-pressure-bias
   rotating case.  Do not initialize pure hydrodynamics from the 0.5 MPa
   pressure-driven velocity field.
4. *Single-liquid onset screen*: continuously ramp rotation and monitor minimum
   absolute pressure, mass balance, load, and torque.  Stop when a phase/rupture
   threshold is crossed; do not force a 2000-rpm single-liquid solution through
   impossible tensile pressure.
5. *Multiphase all-liquid parity*: below onset, the selected multiphase branch
   must reproduce the single-liquid pressure, flow, load, and torque with
   negligible secondary phase.
6. *Onset continuation*: approach the first sustained cavity using smaller
   speed increments and retain initialization/ramp history.
7. *Physical-model sensitivity*: compare the appropriate vapour, air, or
   dissolved-gas interpretation and, for vapour, Schnerr--Sauer versus Zwart.
8. *Numerical sensitivity*: mesh, through-gap resolution, timestep/shaft-angle,
   scheme, and transient-cycle independence.
9. *Quantitative comparison*: pressure profiles, cavity/front location, phase
   volume, boundary flows, load components, and torque against independent
   data.

Passing an earlier gate permits the labels =MESH_ACCEPTED=,
=SETUP_ACCEPTED=, =SCREENING_ONLY=, or =MODEL_SENSITIVITY=.  Use
=QUANTITATIVELY_VALIDATED= only after Gate 9.

*** Pressure convention

Fluent uses:

\[
p_\mathrm{absolute}=p_\mathrm{operating}+p_\mathrm{gauge}.
\]

| Case | Absolute feed pressure | Absolute axial-end pressure |
|---+---:|---:|
| Quiescent or pure hydrodynamic | 101,325 Pa | 101,325 Pa |
| Pressure-fed/hybrid, if =0.5 MPa= is gauge | 601,325 Pa | 101,325 Pa |

For a constant-density vapour-only case, =Operating Pressure = 0 Pa= is the
least ambiguous choice because entered and reported static pressures are then
numerically absolute.  The equivalent =Operating Pressure = 101,325 Pa=
hybrid inputs are =500,000 Pa= inlet gauge total pressure and =0 Pa= outlet
gauge pressure.  An ideal-gas air case should instead use a representative
ambient/mean operating pressure and consistently converted gauge boundaries.
Always plot and monitor absolute pressure explicitly:
[[https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v261/en/flu_ug/flu_ug_sec_op_press.html][Fluent operating-pressure convention]].

Both hybrid forms implement the project's adopted assumption that supply is
0.5 MPa *above atmosphere*.  The source paper states only =Ps=0.5 MPa= and
does not identify its reference.  A Fluent pressure inlet also specifies total
pressure, whereas the reduced feed cells prescribe static pressure.  That
boundary-model mismatch must be reported, especially for a bore patch without
a resolved feed line.

*** Mechanism-specific branches

| Branch | Fluent model | Required physical definition | Claim boundary |
|---+---+---+---|
| =B0= | Single liquid | Exact oil liquid density/viscosity at the adopted temperature and absolute boundaries | Commissioning and pressure/load comparison only |
| =BV= | Mixture, oil primary plus oil vapour secondary; Schnerr--Sauer baseline and Zwart sensitivity | Exact oil and temperature, absolute =p_v(T)=, liquid/vapour density and viscosity, and justified nuclei/model constants | Oil-vapour sensitivity until phase inputs and cavity data are calibrated |
| =BA= | Transient sharp oil--air VOF, no oil--air mass transfer | Real air-access boundary/reservoir, initial and backflow phase fractions, oil--air surface tension, wall contact angle if relevant, and defensible air density law | Ventilation only; it is not oil vapour or dissolved-gas release |
| =BVA= | Implicit three-phase oil--air--oil-vapour formulation, cavitation transfer only oil to/from vapour | Independently accepted =BV= and =BA= branches plus all combined boundaries/properties | Deferred high-cost model, not the first multiphase run |
| Dissolved gas | Calibrated gas-content/solubility/release model | Measured gas content, solubility and release kinetics | Stock Schnerr--Sauer/Zwart oil--vapour transfer is not this mechanism |

For =BV=, initialize from the converged =B0= solution, set oil-feed vapour
fraction to zero, and use zero vapour backflow for a flooded pure-vapour
boundary interpretation.  Fluent's documented =1e11 1/m3= Schnerr--Sauer
bubble-number-density default may be used only as a declared exploratory
baseline, followed by parameter and model-form sensitivity; it is not a
measured =V-32= property.  Use pressure-based coupled treatment and PRESTO,
then check a higher-order volume-fraction/momentum scheme:
[[https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v261/en/flu_ug/flu_ug_sec_multiphase_setup.html][Fluent multiphase setup]].

Robust first-order startup is a project staging choice, not a universal Fluent
recommendation.  A steady =BV= solution is at most a mean cavity map.  Use
transient calculations for growth, collapse, shedding, cycle dependence, and
normally for =BA=.  The current 12 through-gap layers and minimum OQ above
0.9 establish a qualified starting mesh, not adequate VOF-interface
resolution; prove that separately.  Combined VOF plus cavitation should use an
implicit formulation rather than explicit VOF.

Monitor and save:

- global minimum and maximum *absolute* static pressure and their locations;
- saturation vapour pressure and cavitation mass-transfer rate for =BV=;
- total and phase-specific inlet/outlet mass flow rates and imbalance;
- vapour or air volume fractions, thresholded footprint, and integrated cavity
  volume =int(alpha_phase dV)=;
- the specified inlet/backflow phase fractions;
- journal pressure and viscous force components, moment/torque, wall shear,
  and circumferential/axial pressure profiles;
- residual histories plus stable histories of load, torque, flow, and phase
  volume;
- timestep/shaft-angle and cycle-to-cycle periodicity for transient runs;
- mesh, through-gap, timestep, scheme, and model-input sensitivity.

The global minimum need not equal vapour pressure exactly; local numerical
undershoot is possible.  Acceptance comes from bounded, mesh-stable fields and
mass/quantity histories, not from forcing the monitor to land on one number.
Residual convergence alone is insufficient.  Standard cavitation models
predict phase distribution and mass transfer; they do not by themselves
validate erosion risk.  Fluent's phase postprocessing variables and volume
integrals are documented in
[[https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v261/en/flu_ug/flu_ug_sec_multiphase_post.html][multiphase postprocessing]].

** Professor decisions before a predictive multiphase claim

| Decision | Why it changes the model |
|---+---|
| Exact =V-32= product and operating/inlet temperature | Fixes liquid properties, vapour pressure, and any thermal inputs |
| Pure hydrodynamic or pressure-fed/hybrid case | Separates rotation-only pressure generation from the 0.5 MPa supply |
| Whether =0.5 MPa= is gauge or absolute | Changes every absolute pressure and cavitation margin |
| =2.6 m/s= (=496.6 rpm=) or =2000 rpm= | They are inconsistent paper operating points and must not be merged |
| Oil vapour, atmospheric ventilation, dissolved/entrained gas, or JFO rupture | Selects different equations, phases, properties, and validation data |
| Flooded or vented axial ends and backflow phase | Controls replenishment and air/vapour entry |
| Surface bore patch or resolved feed passage/reservoir | Determines whether total-pressure and feed-loss simplifications are acceptable |
| Qualitative visualization/sensitivity or quantitative validation | Determines the required material, experiment, and uncertainty evidence |

** Exact JFO parity inside Fluent: separate solver development

A =DEFINE_CAVITATION_RATE= or =DEFINE_MASS_TRANSFER= UDF changes phase mass
transfer; it does not replace Fluent's pressure equation with the JFO
complementarity system.  Exact parity would require a custom unwrapped
two-dimensional UDS/UDF implementation for pressure/fill, including anisotropic
diffusion, conservative Couette flux, feed constraints, transient storage,
active-set or equivalent complementarity enforcement, and its own benchmark
suite.  Fluent supports custom scalar transport ingredients through
[[https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v261/en/flu_ug/flu_ug_sec_ug_uds.html][user-defined scalars]],
but this is a solver project, not a model-panel setting.

For the final thesis comparison, the shorter defensible route is to keep
OpenFOAM Reynolds/JFO as the reduced film model and Fluent as an independently
validated 3-D vapour or air-ingestion model.  Compare only quantities that mean
the same thing in both.
