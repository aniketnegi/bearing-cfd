* Model

The Reynolds--JFO solver represents the lubricant on an unwrapped conical-film
midsurface.  It solves pressure and fill fraction with an active-set
complementarity method:

\[
p-p_\mathrm{cav}\ge 0,\qquad
0\le \theta\le 1,\qquad
(p-p_\mathrm{cav})(1-\theta)=0.
\]

Full-film cells have \(\theta=1\) and solve the Reynolds pressure equation.
Ruptured cells remain at \(p=p_\mathrm{cav}\) and transport the available
liquid fraction.  This enforces mass conservation through rupture and
reformation without resolving the 20--80 micrometre film thickness in three
dimensions.

The model predicts film pressure, fill, flow, load, and torque under the
thin-film assumptions.  It does not represent oil-vapour bubble dynamics,
atmospheric air transport, thermal viscosity changes, or elastic deformation.

* Published slider check

The Python active-set kernel is tested against the 60-cell sinusoidal slider
reported by Giacopini et al. and plotted in Figure 9 of Miraskari et al.  The
local test uses the incompressible limit with a 125 mm slider, 15--25
micrometre film thickness, 0.015 Pa s viscosity, and 4 m/s sliding speed.

| Outlet pressure | Published graph | Local result |
|---+---+---|
| 0 MPa | peak about 5.9 MPa; rupture near \(x/a=0.65\); no reformation | 5.918 MPa; first cavity-cell centre 0.658; cavity reaches outlet |
| 1 MPa | peak about 6.6 MPa; rupture near 0.65; reformation near 0.95 | 6.618 MPa; first and last cavity-cell centres 0.675 and 0.942 |

Run the check with:

#+begin_src sh
uv run pytest -q \
  tests/conical_journal/test_jfo_published_benchmark.py \
  tests/conical_journal/test_reynolds_jfo.py
#+end_src

The assertions use bands read from the published graph.  They check the
pressure peak and rupture/reformation locations, not the complete pressure

* Gangrade-paper interpretation

The paper supplies enough information to define the bearing dimensions, but
not one unambiguous pressure-feed boundary condition.

| Item | Source statement | Reproduction treatment |
|---+---+---|
| bearing | 100 mm long, 50 mm mean journal radius, 10-degree semi-cone angle, clearance ratio 0.001 | 50 micrometre radial clearance on the same geometry |
| lubricant | dynamic viscosity 0.0277 Pa s; density 860 kg/m3 | used directly and isothermally |
| =p_s= | Table 1 calls 0.5 MPa the supply pressure; pressure is normalized as =p_bar = p/p_s= | 500000 Pa is a reference scale only |
| axial sides | prose calls them atmospheric; Eq. 9b prints =p_bar=1= | 101325 Pa absolute |
| rupture | Eq. 9c uses zero pressure and zero pressure gradient | compare Reynolds/Swift--Stieber with JFO at 101325 Pa absolute rupture pressure |
| oil port | rig Figure 7 labels replenishment port =IP1=; the FEM mesh and boundary equations give no port diameter or feed boundary | retain the project's 4 mm patch at 0 Pa gauge and full liquid; label the diameter as a project assumption |

The source boundary statements are internally inconsistent if =p_bar=p/p_s=:
atmospheric axial edges cannot generally be both =p_bar=1= and a rupture value
of zero, and Figure 9 plots maxima below 0.5 despite Eq. 9b's printed unit
boundary value.  The study therefore does not promote 0.5 MPa to a central
gauge-pressure inlet.

The normalization remains useful:

#+begin_example
load scale = p_s * R_j^2 = 500000 * 0.05^2 = 1250 N
for W_r = 1000 N: W_bar_r = 1000 / 1250 = 0.8
Figure 9 at W_bar_r = 0.8 and gamma = 10 degrees gives p_bar_max about 0.35
p_max about 0.35 * 0.5 MPa = 0.175 MPa
#+end_example

This arithmetic reconciles the paper's roughly 0.17 MPa experimental peak
with its nondimensional load-controlled results without imposing a 0.5 MPa
gauge feed.

The source uses two distinct speeds.  Figure 6 and Figure 8 state 2000 rpm,
whose mean-radius surface speed is 10.472 m/s.  Figures 9 onward state 2.6 m/s,
which equals 496.563 rpm at 50 mm radius.  These operating points are not
interchanged.

* Figure 6 versus Figure 8

Figure 6 is a prescribed-position calculation.  It holds the eccentricity
ratio at each plotted value and reports the resulting normalized maximum
pressure.  Load is an output.  The local sweep covers eccentricity ratios
0.4--0.9 for both pressure-only Reynolds and mass-conserving JFO; 0.9 is an
explicit extrapolation beyond Figure 6's plotted 0.8 endpoint.

Figure 8 is a prescribed-load calculation.  It reports a circumferential
pressure distribution for 1000 N radial load, 200 N axial load, 2000 rpm,
V-32 oil, and 50 micrometre clearance.  Eccentricity magnitude and attitude
cannot be copied from Figure 6: they must be found from equilibrium.  The local
study solves the two in-plane pressure-force equations separately for Reynolds
and JFO:

#+begin_example
F_x = 0 N
F_y = 1000 N
#+end_example

The axial pressure force is then compared with the reported 200 N; it is not a
third fitted target.  The paper does not disclose enough information to map
its plotted zero circumferential angle to the local coordinate system.  The
study records the raw profile and a separately labelled best circular phase
alignment.  The aligned curve is a diagnostic comparison, not predictive
validation.

Both models use the same 4 mm ambient replenishment patch.  With
=feed_gauge_pressure_pa=0=, the patch pressure is atmospheric and its JFO fill
fraction is fixed at one.  This represents oil replacement, not hydrostatic
pressurization.  The controlled comparison changes only the rupture model:

| Model | Unknowns in the rupture region | Mass-conserving reformation |
|---+---+---|
| Reynolds/Swift--Stieber | pressure only; inactive cells remain at rupture pressure | no |
| JFO/Elrod--Adams | rupture pressure and liquid fill fraction below one | yes |

* Feed and grid sensitivity

The uniform-grid solver applies the 4 mm circular feed to cells whose centres
fall inside the nominal radius.  Its represented area changes discontinuously
with resolution:

| Grid | Feed cells | Effective area | Error from \(\pi(2\ \mathrm{mm})^2\) |
|---+---+---+---|
| 128 by 40 | 4 | 24.927 mm2 | +98.4% |
| 192 by 60 | 4 | 11.079 mm2 | -11.8% |
| 256 by 80 | 10 | 15.559 mm2 | +23.8% |
| 384 by 120 | 16 | 11.079 mm2 | -11.8% |
| 512 by 160 | 32 | 12.464 mm2 | -0.8% |

A mapped 512 by 160 run at 2000 rpm changed maximum pressure by -0.932%,
feed flow by +0.486%, and the largest force component by -1.305% relative to
the 256 by 80 result.  This pair is not a grid-convergence study: the feed
areas do not form a consistent refinement family, and the coarser sequence is
non-monotonic.

The body-fitted surface adapter provides a conforming circular rim and matches
the specified feed area within 0.1% in its regression test.  A complete
three-grid solution study using that representation has not been run.

* Remaining validation

1. Use one feed geometry across at least three systematically refined grids.
2. Report pressure, load components, feed flow, torque, rupture boundary, and
   liquid deficit with observed-order or GCI estimates.
3. Exercise the native OpenFOAM conical assembly against a manufactured or
   published field, not only the Python active-set kernel.
4. Report local control-volume imbalance and complementarity residuals in
   addition to global flow balance.
5. Compare the complete pressure distribution with experimental taps or an
   independent numerical dataset.
6. Resolve lubricant properties, rupture pressure, feed-pressure convention,
   and flooded versus vented axial boundaries.

Until these checks are complete, the conical results are numerical sensitivity
data rather than a validated bearing-performance prediction.
